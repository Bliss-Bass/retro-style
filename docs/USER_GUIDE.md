# UI style

Choose how Android looks and where the bottom bar's controls sit.

Open **Settings > System > UI style**. Changes apply right away. When a change needs SystemUI to
restart, the screen redraws once and your open windows come back where they were.

## Style

Styles come in three groups. Your device may have fewer: the people who built it choose which to
include.

**Today's Android**

- **Material You**: today's Android look, with colors from your wallpaper.
- **Material 3**: the same look with fixed colors that don't follow the wallpaper.

**Android through the years**

- **Froyo** and **Gingerbread**: Android 2.x. The original grey buttons and checkboxes on a
  black background, with orange (Froyo) or green (Gingerbread) highlights.
- **Honeycomb**: Android 3, the first tablet Android. Holo, with deep navy panels.
- **Jelly Bean** and **KitKat**: the Android 4.x tablet look. Black and grey with Holo blue
  accents (pure black needs a LineageOS build), square corners, Holo buttons and switches, the
  navigation icons of the time, and Quick Settings shown as a list of ON/OFF rows under a large
  clock. Jelly Bean has the blue status icons; KitKat the later glyphs.
- **Holo Light**: the light Android 4.x look.
- **Lollipop**: Android 5, the first Material Design. Light, flat, indigo.
- **Pie**: Android 9. Rounded cards and Google blue, before Material You.

**ROM and community classics**

- **Pitch Black**: pure black backgrounds, good for OLED screens (LineageOS builds).
- **Glass**: see-through Notifications and Quick Settings panels and system bar.
- **Mono**: greys only.
- **Nord**, **Dracula**, **Solarized Dark**, **Gruvbox** and **Catppuccin Mocha**: popular color
  schemes from code editors and desktops.

Some styles turn dark mode on (the Holo and classic ones, and most community styles) or off
(Holo Light, Lollipop). Styles that leave it alone, like Material You, give you your own dark
mode setting back.

**KitKat panels** picks the panel color for the styles with KitKat panels that don't set their
own: **KitKat stock** (black) or **Holo unified** (navy).

Apps restyle the next time they open. Some apps draw their own look; they still take the
colors, fonts and dark mode, but keep their own buttons and shapes.

## Bottom bar

- **Taskbar** (the usual default): the normal taskbar.
- **Tablet system bar**: one bar across the bottom, laid out like Android 3.x/4.x tablets.
  - On the left: back, home and recents, plus any extra buttons you turn on.
  - In the middle: your apps and running tasks, if you turn them on.
  - On the right, the tray: a bell (or your notification count or icons), the status icons from
    the top of the screen (Bluetooth, alarm, Do Not Disturb, location and so on), network and
    battery, volume, and the clock.
  - Tap the bell to see your notifications; tap the status icons or the clock for Quick Settings.

The tablet system bar works with every style.

### Tablet system bar options

- **Show apps and running tasks**: your pinned apps and open apps between the buttons and the
  tray. Off at first for the tablet-era styles (Honeycomb, Jelly Bean, KitKat), on for the
  others, until you change it.
- **Apps on the tablet system bar**: right after the buttons, centered (default), or just before
  the tray. They never cover the buttons or the tray.
- **Background on the home screen**: **Stock** (as the launcher draws it), **Solid**, **Shadow
  gradient** (the Android 4.x look: clear at the top, dark at the bottom), **Semi-transparent** or
  **Transparent**. Some styles pick one for you (KitKat the gradient, for example) until you
  choose. In apps the bar keeps its usual background.
- **Extra buttons**: volume down, volume up, screenshot and sleep (hold it for the power menu),
  each with its own switch. All off at first.

## Notifications and Quick Settings open

- **From the top right**: under the status bar, as on Chromebooks.
- **From the tray, bottom right**: above the bar, like the old tablets. Swipe down on a panel to
  close it. This is the default with the tablet system bar.

## Hide the top status bar

With the tablet system bar, you can hide the status bar at the top of the screen; its icons stay
in the tray. Styles with KitKat panels (Honeycomb, Jelly Bean, KitKat) always hide it.
